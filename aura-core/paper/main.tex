\begin{abstract}
Advising university students on institutional academic regulations requires absolute factual fidelity, zero hallucination tolerance, and strict data privacy. Conventional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) implementations typically rely on single-stage vector similarity searches wrapped in heavy runtime frameworks, which frequently introduce semantic drift, clause confusion, and hallucinated regulatory allowances. This paper presents \textbf{AURA Core}, a sovereign, high-performance, and lightweight Pure-Go native Two-Stage RAG architecture tailored specifically for institutional academic governance. The system integrates a first-stage Dense Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) vector retrieval (top-20 candidates using 1024-dimensional multilingual embeddings) coupled with a second-stage Cross-Encoder Neural Reranker (top-8 candidates) and low-temperature reasoning with mandatory article-level citation constraints. Furthermore, student academic transcripts (KHS/KRS) are parsed strictly in-memory per session, guaranteeing zero persistence in public vector stores. We rigorously evaluate the architecture using \textbf{UII-Bench-50} (a benchmark of 50 institutional scenarios across five regulatory clusters) and 15 comprehensive student lifecycle study cases scored via automated LLM-as-Judge RAG Triad metrics (Context Relevance, Groundedness, and Answer Relevance). Experimental results demonstrate that AURA Core achieves an overall Context Relevance of 87.40\%, Groundedness of 91.80\%, Answer Relevance of 98.60\%, and a composite Triad Score of 90.55\% with a mean end-to-end latency under 5.0 seconds. A controlled ablation study proves that Stage-2 Neural Reranking is critical, conferring a +10.58\% leap in Groundedness over the single-stage baseline (81.22\% vs. 91.80\%) at an incremental computational overhead of only $\approx$330 ms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Navigating higher education regulations—such as thesis proposal prerequisites, semester credit limits (\textit{Satuan Kredit Semester} or SKS), supervisor decree validity, Turnitin similarity thresholds, and graduation requirements—constitutes a critical yet error-prone dimension of university academic governance \cite{uii2024pedoman, fti2024pedoman}. In institutional settings, advisory errors do not merely cause confusion; an erroneous approval of an ineligible thesis proposal or an unverified semester overload can result in student deregistration or administrative sanction.

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) have offered promising pathways toward automated student consultation \cite{lewis2020retrieval, gao2023retrieval}. However, standard naive RAG pipelines present three severe vulnerabilities when deployed in rigorous regulatory domains:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Semantic Drift in Single-Stage ANN}: High-dimensional cosine similarity searches frequently retrieve adjacent or generic policy passages rather than the exact binding clause, causing the LLM to synthesize superficially plausible yet legally invalid advice (hallucinations) \cite{ji2023survey, huang2023survey}.
    \item \textbf{Heavy Runtime Overhead and Vendor Coupling}: Most contemporary academic AI prototypes rely on bulky Python frameworks (e.g., LangChain, LlamaIndex), creating deployment fragility, high memory footprints, and architectural vendor lock-in.
    \item \textbf{Student Data Privacy Risks}: Academic advising requires parsing sensitive student transcripts (\textit{Kartu Hasil Studi} or KHS). Inadvertent ingestion of personalized records into communal vector databases creates severe data leakage across student cohorts.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, this paper introduces \textbf{AURA Core} (\textit{Academic Universal Regulatory Assistant}), a sovereign Pure-Go native Two-Stage RAG system engineered for the Faculty of Industrial Technology at Universitas Islam Indonesia (FTI UII). Developed under an institutional research grant, AURA Core orchestrates a complete native pipeline without relying on external Python runtimes. It enforces strict two-stage retrieval—combining 1024-dimensional dense Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) retrieval with a Cross-Encoder Neural Reranker—to guarantee that only authoritative regulatory articles enter the generation prompt context.

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We design and implement a sovereign, concurrent Pure-Go native orchestrator that unifies vector retrieval, cross-encoder neural reranking, streaming token generation, and multi-session persistence.
    \item We establish \textbf{UII-Bench-50}, an institutional benchmark comprising 50 institutional scenarios spanning five core regulatory clusters, evaluated via an automated LLM-as-Judge RAG Triad framework.
    \item We conduct an empirical \textbf{Ablation Study} comparing Single-Stage (ANN-only) versus Two-Stage (ANN + Cross-Encoder) pipelines, proving that neural reranking elevates Groundedness from 81.22\% to 91.80\% (+10.58\% gain) with negligible latency cost ($\approx$330 ms).
    \item We validate the system across 15 real-world student lifecycle cases (CS01–CS15), demonstrating robust boundary constraint enforcement, adversarial fictitious rule rejection, and out-of-domain isolation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)}
RAG architectures combine parametric memory stored in LLM weights with non-parametric memory retrieved dynamically from external corpora \cite{lewis2020retrieval, karpukhin2020dense}. While dense bi-encoders enable scalable vector similarity searches via Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) indexing, they compress document passages into fixed-length vectors, often losing delicate syntactical constraints such as cumulative versus alternative legal conditions \cite{reimers2019sentence}.

\subsection{Cross-Encoder Neural Re-ranking}
To mitigate loss of fine-grained semantic nuance, two-stage retrieval cascades introduce Cross-Encoder architectures \cite{nogueira2019passage}. Unlike bi-encoders, cross-encoders feed the query and candidate passage simultaneously into full cross-attention layers, computing precise token-level interactions. While computationally prohibitive for searching millions of records, applying a cross-encoder to a pre-filtered candidate subset (e.g., top-20 to top-8) provides an optimal Pareto trade-off between retrieval recall and precision \cite{gao2023retrieval}.

\subsection{Hallucination Mitigation in Institutional AI}
Hallucination in NLG refers to generated content that is nonsensical or unfaithful to the source document \cite{ji2023survey, shuster2021retrieval}. In university regulatory environments, hallucination mitigation requires strict \textit{factual grounding}: the model must refuse to extrapolate beyond explicit codified clauses \cite{asai2023self}. Frameworks such as the RAG Triad (Context Relevance, Groundedness, and Answer Relevance) pioneered by TruLens \cite{saad2023trulens} and Ragas \cite{es2023ragas} provide formalized automated metrics to evaluate this compliance.

\subsection{Two-Stage Retrieval Cascade}
\textbf{Stage 1 (Dense Vector Retrieval):} Given an incoming student query $q$, AURA Core computes a 1024-dimensional dense semantic embedding vector $\mathbf{v}_q = f_{\text{embed}}(q)$ using multilingual representation models. A dense ANN query is executed against the regulatory index $\mathcal{I}_{\text{reg}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{20} = \text{Top-}K_{\text{ANN}}(\mathbf{v}_q, \mathcal{I}_{\text{reg}}, K=20)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Stage 2 (Cross-Encoder Neural Reranking):} Because dense vector embeddings can rank superficially similar paragraphs higher than binding regulatory clauses, the candidate pool $\mathcal{C}_{20} = \{p_1, \dots, p_{20}\}$ is fed into a cross-encoder reranker $g_{\text{rerank}}(q, p_i)$. Each candidate is re-scored via deep cross-attention:
\begin{equation}
s_i = g_{\text{rerank}}(q, p_i), \quad \forall p_i \in \mathcal{C}_{20}
\end{equation}
The candidates are sorted descending by $s_i$, and the top $N=8$ most authoritative passages are retained:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}_8 = \text{Top-}N(\mathcal{C}_{20}, s, N=8)
\end{equation}

\subsection{In-Memory Privacy-Preserving Student Transcript Parser}
A unique architectural mandate of AURA Core is absolute isolation of student academic transcripts. When a student uploads a KHS or KRS (in PDF or tabular format), the file is processed strictly \textit{in-memory} via localized stream extraction. The extracted transcript features (completed credits, current GPA, semester grade distribution) are injected solely into the ephemeral session context of that specific chat session. Student records are \textbf{never embedded, indexed, or stored in the communal vector database}, entirely eliminating cross-user data leakage.

\subsection{Anti-Hallucination Reasoning and Citation Enforcement}
The final context $\mathcal{X} = \{\mathcal{P}_8, \mathcal{T}_{\text{student}}\}$ is passed to the generation model with temperature $\tau = 0.2$. System prompts enforce three non-negotiable guardrails:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Clause Grounding}: Claims must be directly supported by retrieved text; extrapolating uncodified leniency or non-existent exemptions is strictly prohibited.
    \item \textbf{Explicit Refusal on Missing Clauses}: If a query concerns regulations outside the official knowledge base (e.g., non-FTI faculties), the model must explicitly declare absence of documentation rather than guess.
    \item \textbf{Mandatory Article Citation}: Every prescriptive statement must cite the source title, chapter, and article number (e.g., \textit{[Pedoman Akademik UII, Pasal 10]}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Formalization of RAG Triad Evaluation Metrics}
To measure retrieval fidelity and generation faithfulness quantitatively, we employ the RAG Triad framework formulated through automated LLM-as-Judge scoring \cite{es2023ragas, saad2023trulens}:

\textbf{1) Context Relevance (CR):} Measures the proportion of retrieved passages $\mathcal{P}$ that are directly relevant to query $q$:
\begin{equation}
\text{CR}(q, \mathcal{P}) \in [0.0, 1.0]
\end{equation}

\textbf{2) Groundedness (G):} Measures the factual faithfulness of generated answer $a$ relative to retrieved passages $\mathcal{P}$. Every claim $c \in a$ must be entailed by $\mathcal{P}$:
\begin{equation}
\text{G}(\mathcal{P}, a) = \frac{|\{c \in a : \mathcal{P} \models c\}|}{|\{c \in a\}|} \in [0.0, 1.0]
\end{equation}

\textbf{3) Answer Relevance (AR):} Measures how directly and completely answer $a$ addresses the user's information need $q$:
\begin{equation}
\text{AR}(q, a) \in [0.0, 1.0]
\end{equation}

\textbf{4) RAG Triad Score (RTS):} Computed as the harmonic mean of the three constituent metrics, heavily penalizing any failure in Groundedness or Relevance:
\begin{equation}
\text{RTS} = \frac{3}{\frac{1}{\text{CR}} + \frac{1}{\text{G}} + \frac{1}{\text{AR}}}
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Institutional Regulatory Knowledge Base}
The corpus comprises authoritative regulatory documents published by Universitas Islam Indonesia and the Faculty of Industrial Technology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Buku Pedoman Akademik Universitas Islam Indonesia} (Academic Handbook, 38 Articles).
    \item \textit{Buku Pedoman Pelaksanaan Tugas Akhir \& Skripsi FTI UII} (Undergraduate Thesis Handbook, 24 Articles).
    \item \textit{Panduan Beasiswa DPK \& DPPAI UII} (Student Financial Aid Guidelines).
    \item \textit{Informasi Kontak Resmi \& Akreditasi Institusi FTI UII}.
\end{itemize}
Documents were segmented into semantic chunks of 500--800 tokens with 100-token overlaps, preserving section and clause metadata.

\subsection{The UII-Bench-50 Dataset}
To benchmark institutional RAG performance rigorously, we designed \textbf{UII-Bench-50}, a standardized dataset containing 50 institutional scenarios categorized into five regulatory clusters:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cluster 1: Thesis Proposal Prerequisites} (10 scenarios; minimum SKS, cumulative GPA, grade E restrictions, research methodology grade).
    \item \textbf{Cluster 2: Semester Credit Limits \& IPS} (10 scenarios; GPA-to-SKS distribution, short-semester limits, overload requests).
    \item \textbf{Cluster 3: Supervisor Decrees \& Advisory Limits} (10 scenarios; decree duration, extension limits, consultation logs).
    \item \textbf{Cluster 4: Turnitin \& Scientific Integrity} (10 scenarios; similarity index limits, exclusions, sanctions).
    \item \textbf{Cluster 5: Degree Conferral \& Graduation} (10 scenarios; total 144 SKS, CEPT English threshold, D-grade allowance, DO evaluation).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis of Critical Edge Cases}
Four representative cases illuminate the qualitative robustness of AURA Core:

\textbf{1) Maximum Constraint Enforcement (CS05):} When a student with a near-perfect GPA (3.95) requested 26 SKS, naive models frequently concede due to prompt flattery. AURA Core strictly rejected the request, citing \textit{Pasal 10 Buku Pedoman Akademik}, explaining that 24 SKS is the unalterable system boundary, while clarifying the legal distinction between semester GPA (IPS) and cumulative GPA (IPK).

\textbf{2) Adversarial Fictitious Rule Injection:} When asked whether a thesis proposal exemption could be granted via a recommendation letter from a local village chief (Ketua RT), AURA Core immediately declared that no such mechanism exists in university policy. It recited the six cumulative requirements of Article 2 and astutely clarified that village poverty letters (SKTM) relate exclusively to student welfare aid (DPK), not academic prerequisite waivers.

\textbf{3) Nuanced Regulatory Clause Interpretation (CS03):} In evaluating a previously failed course (Grade E) that was subsequently repeated and replaced with Grade A, AURA Core demonstrated epistemic precision: it explained that if the grade E is no longer present on the active official transcript, the student is eligible, while honestly advising the student to verify their transcript rendering with the academic division.

\textbf{4) Out-of-Domain (OOD) Scope Boundary Awareness:} When queried regarding CEPT score requirements for the Faculty of Medicine (FK UII), AURA Core refrained from hallucinating medical faculty policies. It stated that its authoritative index covers FTI UII regulations (where the requirement is 450), and escalated the inquiry directly to the university's Division of Academic Administration (DAA).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The deployment of AURA Core highlights the necessity of architectural sovereignty for institutional AI. By orchestrating RAG natively in Go, memory allocations are tightly bounded, enabling continuous production availability on modest virtual private servers. 

\textbf{Threats to Validity:} While LLM-as-Judge evaluation with full passage context provides reproducible and unbiased scoring, minor stochastic variations in LLM inference remain possible. To mitigate this, temperature was fixed at $\tau = 0.0$ for evaluation judges. Future iterations will integrate fine-grained hybrid lexical-dense retrieval (BM25 + Dense ANN) to further elevate Context Relevance in rare acronym searches.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented AURA Core, a sovereign Pure-Go native Two-Stage RAG architecture designed for institutional academic advising at the Faculty of Industrial Technology, Universitas Islam Indonesia. By cascading dense vector ANN retrieval with Cross-Encoder Neural Reranking and enforcing in-memory student record isolation, the system effectively neutralizes regulatory hallucinations. Across 50 benchmark scenarios and 15 student lifecycle cases, AURA Core achieved 87.40\% Context Relevance, 91.80\% Groundedness, and 98.60\% Answer Relevance. Our ablation study proved that neural reranking contributes a pivotal +10.58\% gain in Groundedness over single-stage architectures with negligible latency overhead. AURA Core establishes a validated, privacy-preserving reference architecture for deploying reliable AI advising systems in higher education.
